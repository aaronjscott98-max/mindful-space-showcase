Understanding Depression

Depression is often described as moving through dense, heavy air. It is not a temporary dip in mood, but a profound state of exhaustion that affects motivation, perception, and physical energy.

The Hidden Impact

Beyond sadness, depression often manifests as "cognitive fog," where simple decisions feel like monumental tasks. It can dampen physical sensations, making the world seem colourless or distant. Recognising this as a health state rather than a personal failure is the first step toward effective support.

A gentle reminder

You can take things one small step at a time. Seeking help is a valid next step.

Navigating Daily Life

Depression often strips away the "sense of tomorrow." Support can be found in helping to break the day into much smaller, manageable segments. A success can simply be making a cup of tea, stepping outside for five minutes, or responding to a single message. Acknowledging these small "wins" provides a powerful, gentle reminder of progress.

Reframing the Narrative

Common Myth: If someone is smiling or laughing, they cannot be depressed.

The Reality: Depression often involves "masking," where an individual puts significant effort into appearing "fine" for the sake of others. The absence of outward sorrow does not equate to the absence of internal struggle.

How to Provide Support

  • Offer Specific Help: Avoid general questions. Offer specific tasks: "I will be over at 2:00 PM to help with the washing up," or "I am going to the shop; what can I pick up for the fridge?"
  • Approaching Conversations: Focus on connection. Instead of asking "Why do you feel this way?", try "I value your company and I am happy to just sit here with you for a while."
  • Reduce the Pressure to Perform: There is no requirement to be cheerful. Allow for silence and presence without the need for forced conversation.
  • Prioritise Small Steps: Celebrate small, functional achievements without making the person feel pressured to "get better" immediately.
  • Patient Consistency: The most valuable support is reliable, long-term presence. Simply checking in without placing demands shows that they are not forgotten.

Finding Support and Safety

If someone is at immediate risk of harm, please contact emergency services without delay:

  • Emergency: Call 999 or visit the nearest Accident and Emergency department.
  • Urgent Medical Advice: Call 111 to reach NHS 24.
  • Samaritans: Call 116 123 (Available 24/7).
  • Breathing Space (Scotland): Call 0800 83 85 87 (Mon–Thu 6 PM–2 AM; Fri 6 PM–Mon 6 AM).
